# horizon-porter 0.9.2

More of each mod's Java comes over, a port builds on whichever Horizon you have, and every port
now says in plain words what the translation left out.

**Re-port your mods with this version** (`port --force`, or Port again in the app), then
`translate --refresh`.

## Ports build on any recent Horizon

0.9.1 wrote the sound and particle calls for the Horizon it found on your computer. A port now
carries both forms and the compiler picks the one that fits, so the same port builds on an
instance that plays an older Horizon and on the newest nightly. horizon-porter's tests check both.

## What ported code can reach now

- **Sturdy faces** (`isFaceSturdy`, `canSupportCenter`, `canSupportRigidBlock`): what a block
  that rests on or hangs from another asks. From the block's shape where Horizon gives it.
- **The held item while placing** (`context.getItemInHand()`): a block that takes its colour or
  kind from its item.
- **Action-bar messages** (`displayClientMessage(text, true)`) and **breaking a block with its
  drops** (`level.destroyBlock`).
- **The mod's config, when it is a class of plain fields**: Cloth/AutoConfig, OmegaConfig,
  MidnightLib and the like, which most Fabric mods use. The values become the port's `Config`,
  kept in Horizon's mod settings with a page in Options > Mods. Beautify, Villagers Plus (49
  values) and Friends & Foes (86) get theirs.
- On Horizon's new mod API, a block placed in water knows it, and waterlogs as it does in Java.
- **Spawning entities** (`type.spawn`, `addFreshEntity`): from a click, an item, a block
  entity's tick and a block's own tick. Babies (`setAge(-24000)`) and thrown things
  (`shoot`, `setDeltaMovement`) too. An egg block hatches now.
- **Blocks that remove themselves** when a neighbour changes (`updateShape` returning air) and
  ones that schedule a tick there. The most common thing ports said they couldn't do.
- **Where the player stands, their eyes and the way they look** in a use; vanilla's **level
  events** (`levelEvent`), a block's **break effect** and its **own particles**
  (`BlockParticleOption`), **game events** for sculk, **sounds at a point**, whether a spot
  **sees the sky**, and the sky's and the blocks' light apart.

## Things you throw

A mod's thrown item (`ThrowableItemProjectile`: Farmer's Delight's rotten tomato, Naturalist's
duck egg) is now one of Horizon's thrown entity types, drawn as its item. 0.9.1 left every
projectile out. Its item's Java `use` is ported on top, so it flies the way the Java throws it,
with the mod's own sound. Golems and fish that the porter skipped as "not a mob" come over too.

Checked in game on regular Horizon: a rotten tomato thrown along the look, a pie popping off
when the block under it goes, and an ostrich egg cracking, hatching and leaving a baby ostrich.

## Five mods it had never seen

To check this isn't tuned to the mods above, five others went through the whole flow untouched:
port, translate with Claude Haiku, build, play.

| Mod | Functions ported | Cost | Blocks that place | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Animal Feeding Trough | 6 of 6 | $0.03 | 1 of 1 | plays |
| Enchanting Infuser | 7 of 7 | $0.05 | 2 of 2 | plays |
| Trading Post | 5 of 5 | $0.03 | 1 of 1 | plays |
| GraveStone | 16 of 16 | $0.13 | 1 of 1 | plays |
| Waystones | 68 of 68 | $0.79 | 31 of 31 | plays |

"Plays" means it builds, loads and its blocks place and stand. It does not mean the mod is whole:
what these five are for mostly sits where Horizon has no hook for ported code yet. A trading
post gathers the villagers around it, a grave is made when a player dies, a waystone opens its
own screen to teleport. Each port's TRANSLATION-NOTES.md says so function by function.

Two things they asked for are in this version: **a line in the player's chat**
(`sendSystemMessage`), and **the ids behind a mod's registry constants** (`ModSounds.EGG_HATCH`)
when that class is too long to show the translator.

They also turned up three faults that are fixed here:

- **Blocks registered in a loop** (Waystones' `andesite_waystone`, `mossy_waystone`...) were found
  by their resources but never tied to their Java class, so none of its code ran for them. A
  block found that way now takes the class its name ends with (`WaystoneBlock`): a waystone
  stands two blocks high, and loses its top when its base is broken.
- **A block entity shared by two blocks of one class** (Enchanting Infuser's plain and advanced
  infusers) went only to the block of the type's name. Both open now.
- **A two-block-tall block's "is the spot above free"** (`canBeReplaced(context)`) was translated
  as "refuse when holding this block", which refused every placement. Ported code has
  `world.replaceable(pos)` for it now.

## Found by playing the ports

A round of hands-on play turned up more, all fixed for any mod that does the same:

- **A screen from a library's base class** (Enchanting Infuser's
  `AbstractWidgetsContainerScreen<InfuserMenu>`, sized by `super(.., 220, 185)`) wasn't linked to
  its menu, so it opened as a short blank panel with the inventory hanging below it. The screen's
  menu is read from its own `extends`, its size from the `super` call, the inventory from
  `addStandardInventorySlots`, and armour and off-hand slots no longer pass for the inventory.
  A screen whose size still isn't known is made as large as its inventory needs.
- **A function ported while its Java wasn't found** is ported again once it is.
- **Multi-face blocks** (`MultifaceBlock`: Naturalist's starfish) took a plain stone template, so
  no face was ever set and nothing was drawn. They copy glow lichen now.
- **A placement built on the vanilla one** (`super.getStateForPlacement(context)` and then a
  tweak) threw the vanilla part away. Ported code returns `SUPER` to keep the template's own
  placement and set its properties on top.
- **`.noCollision()`** is honoured whatever the template (a stone copy was solid), and
  **`.lightLevel(SomeBlock.FIELD)`** is read from the function the field holds. Naturalist's glow
  goop is an invisible light source in Java: ours was invisible, solid and dark.

## On Horizon's mod API fork

`port --api` now targets the fork's Nightly of 2026-11-10 (`34724f7`), which is playable where
the earlier prebuilt wasn't. Through its API, ported code reaches what regular Horizon's hooks
don't give yet:

- **Stats, item cooldowns, effects on the player, whether they can eat** (`awardStat`,
  `addCooldown`, `addEffect`, `canEat`).
- **A ray from the player's eyes** (`getPlayerPOVHitResult`, `level.clip`) and **the mobs near a
  spot** (`getEntitiesOfClass`), with love mode for a feeder.
- **What a block does when a player places or breaks it** (`setPlacedBy`,
  `playerWillDestroy`), which the API build left out before.
- **A dying player's drops** (`LivingDropsEvent`): GraveStone's grave. The mod's handling of
  the event is ported as one function; a block entity that keeps items inside a class of its own
  (the grave's `Death`) gets item slots for them.

Played on that Nightly with the twelve trial mods together: a waystone placed by hand stands two
blocks high, and a player who dies from a fall leaves a grave holding what they carried, which
gives it back once when broken. A death by `/kill` leaves none: the fork doesn't tell the hook
of it yet.

## A first step into Create

Create's 1.21.1 source, run through as an experiment on a developer's own copy
([docs/CREATE.md](../CREATE.md)). All 646 of its blocks place, and a creative motor turns shafts
and meshing cogwheels, each the way Create turns it, and stops them when it is taken away: four
functions translated from Create's Java, nothing by hand. Create is not
ported (625 functions wait, and stress, belts, fluids and contraptions each need more), but what
it took helps any mod built like it:

- **A block entity inherits** its fields, saved values and tick from the mod's own superclasses.
- **A tick sees its neighbours' state** (`machine.neighbour(side).state("axis")`), and a
  renderer its own block's (`frame.state("axis")`).
- **A walk over a network becomes each block's own step.** Where the Java visits every connected
  block entity in one go (rotation, energy, a signal), the tick is told to work out its share from
  the six blocks beside it, so a change travels a block a tick.
- **The mod's own interfaces are answered.** Where code asks a block it meets a question
  (`IRotate.hasShaftTowards`), the prompt lists what each block class answers.
- **`getRenderShape`** reaches the block's file: a block its block entity draws keeps its model
  out of the world.
- **Renderers found through a registrar** (`.renderer(() -> X::new)`) and **partial models named
  through a helper** (`block("shaft_half")`): Create goes from 4 render functions to 101, and
  from 20 baked part models to 245.
- **A `BlockPos` field** of a block entity is kept (as text).
- **Item icons for a mod from before 1.21.4**, which ships no `items/*.json`: a definition is
  written for each of its item models.

`getRenderShape` changes three blocks of the trial mods too, as Java has them: Naturalist's glow
goop and Waystones' fleeting memorial draw no model, and Naturalist's snail shell is drawn by
its renderer alone, where the port drew it twice.

## The translator is told what it has

A long list of abilities gets overlooked: a re-port of Naturalist's duck egg still said it had no
way to award a stat with `world.stat` in front of it. The prompt now names, beside each Java call
the function makes, the call that stands for it (`player.awardStat(..)` is `world.stat(..)`),
and asks for a working approximation over doing nothing: Naturalist's hedgehog is thrown without
its saved name and health rather than not at all.

## The check places blocks the way a player does

`horizon-porter check` put every block down with `/setblock`, which never runs a block's own
placement code, so it missed that last fault. It now also places each block that has ported
placement code by hand, with its item, and says how many stood: `135 of 135 blocks place, 34 of
35 respond to a click, 36 of 38 place by hand`. A block that needs water or something to hang
from is listed as a note, not a fault.

## Refresh costs less

`translate --refresh` is selective now: a function ported before is redone only when its notes
name something this version gives it. Bringing Farmer's Delight up to date took 89 functions with
0.9.1; from 0.9.1 to this version it is a fraction of that.

## TRANSLATION-NOTES.md

After translating, the port folder has a `TRANSLATION-NOTES.md`: each ported function that
couldn't do part of what its Java does, and what that part is, in the translation's own words.
